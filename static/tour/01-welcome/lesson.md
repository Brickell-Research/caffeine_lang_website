**Caffeine** is a programming language for generating reliability artifacts from service expectation definitions.

We built Caffeine to aid reliability concious software operators in the era of highly dynamic systems, aligning with the views of [Chad Fowler's Phoenix Architecture](https://aicoding.leaflet.pub/), [Google's evolution of SRE](https://www.usenix.org/publications/loginonline/evolution-sre-google), the system thinking outlined by [Donella Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking_In_Systems:_A_Primer) and [Nancy Leveson](http://sunnyday.mit.edu/STAMP-publications.html), the ["test in prod" mindset of Charity Majors](https://increment.com/testing/i-test-in-production/), and many others.

Our definition of Caffeine above is intentionaly vague, affording us a wide domain beyond the initial SLO focused use case, capturing the broader vision of the language.

Defined below are the core inputs and outputs of Caffeine:
* _service expectation_: a declaration asserting some property of a system, either at the component or greater system level.
* _reliability artifact_: an object generated by the Caffeine compiler enabling verification, either automatically or manually, of a system property.
